I've been really interested in our use of the word "world" in conversations about adding content to the common ground, assertion, etc. Specifically, the idea that you can explain the kind of world you believe you live in (the way you view the world at any given moment) through assertion. I think of it like having a block of stone that represents all possible worlds, but every sentence is a chip at the block that reveals the globe statue in the middle.

Anyways, my experience doing improvisational comedy at UChicago has touched on this idea in an opposite way - "world building" as opposed to "narrowing" down the common ground to expose the most likely worlds. For example, in an improv scene, my scene partner could begin by saying "the sky's looking mighty brown today!" in a really chipper voice, and it would be my job to realize that brown is an amazing and normal color of the sky in this world we are building. Then imagine that someone walks onto the scene and goes, "Ugh, I wish the sky were blue today". It would be my job, as part of the established world, to say something like "why would you want the sky to be blue? That seems unnatural and gross, we should be thankful for the vibrant brown that it is today!". In a way, I'm calling out someone else's "bullshit" by recognizing that their behavior goes against the rules of the world we've built - no regard for the truth of this world.

I give this example to say that testing a hypothesis, taking a sentence seriously to find all of its blind spots, paradoxes, falsities, etc, is a core part of what makes improv funny. But bullshit must be spewed before the sentence can be exploited. One very abstract, undefined idea I had is a "world-building" game that takes a tweet, a sentence, an opinion you overheard, etc, and hypothesizes how the world would have to look for that statement to be true. This could be an exercise for an LLM, or for a human, so that they can judge if the worlds they're building really do hold true to the world they think they live in. If the world they end up creating seems somehow ridiculous, paradoxical, or just plain awful.... then maybe they deem the sentence as bullshit. Or... maybe not?

In some ways, the above proposal is more of a **motivation,** which has a lot of impractical elements to it (what questions do we ask? how many? would this work if someone had a messed up world view?, who would actually use this...?), but I'm open to suggestions on implementation, goal, or anything else!!
