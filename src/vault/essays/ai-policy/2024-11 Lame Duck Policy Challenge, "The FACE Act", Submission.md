**The FACE (Fighting Artificial Child-image Exploitation) Act**

**Sponsored by**: Senator Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) and Senator Ed Markey (D-MA)

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States in Congress assembled.

**SECTION 1. PREAMBLE**

In the 1982 landmark case, New York v. [Ferber](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/458/747/), the Supreme Court asserted the unanimous opinion that “the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse of children constitutes a government objective of surpassing importance.” Furthermore, the U.S. Surgeon General has identified child exposure to sexual content as a contributor to our national youth mental health crisis in his recent [Advisory on Social Media and Youth Mental Health](https://www.hhs.gov/about/news/2023/05/23/surgeon-general-issues-new-advisory-about-effects-social-media-use-has-youth-mental-health.html). The rapid advancement of generative AI has magnified these risks by accelerating the creation and spread of sexually explicit deepfakes.

The Biden Administration has taken decisive action on AI governance through its [Executive Order on Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development of AI](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/presidential-actions/2023/10/30/executive-order-on-the-safe-secure-and-trustworthy-development-and-use-of-artificial-intelligence/), emphasizing the need to protect Americans' privacy and civil liberties in the AI-age. Since then, the administration has issued a [Call to Action](https://www.whitehouse.gov/ostp/news-updates/2024/05/23/a-call-to-action-to-combat-image-based-sexual-abuse/) to combat image-based sexual abuse and has launched [initiatives through the DOJ, DOE, NSF, and NIH](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2024/10/30/fact-sheet-key-ai-accomplishments-in-the-year-since-the-biden-harris-administrations-landmark-executive-order/) on deepfake detection and deepfake victim support services.

[Children’s online health and safety has also been an significant part of the administration’s digital governance agenda](https://www.whitehouse.gov/ostp/news-updates/2024/07/22/biden-harris-administration-takes-actions-to-advance-kids-online-health-safety-and-privacy/); under the Biden administration the FTC, DHS, and NIST have taken action in areas such as age verification, privacy enforcement, online image-based sexual abuse, and sexual exploitation. Despite the Administration's advocacy for Congressional action on children's online safety, comprehensive legislation is still lacking.

The FACE Act aims to combat harms caused by explicit deepfakes towards children given their increasing ubiquity and ease of creation. The FACE Act mandates stringent measures to detect child pornography in the deep fake engines, enforces the modification of existing open-source contribution guidelines and standards to exclude open-source projects which enable the creation of child pornography, and ensures that deepfake technologies are inaccessible to children under 13.

**SECTION 2. RESTRICTIONS ON CREATION OF SYNTHETIC MEDIA INVOLVING MINORS**

**(a) Sexually Explicit Child Content Detection in Existing Synthetic Content Engines:**

While many existing engines have strict prompt filters for explicit content, explicit content is still capable of being obtained through hallucination or strategically written prompts. Existing publicly available deepfake or AI-media creation engines must employ content detection systems that identify 1) sexually explicit content and 2) the appearance of children in the deepfake and image generation process. Existing technologies such as Google Cloud’s Explicit Content Detection software (Safe Search), Microsfot Azure’s AI Vision, or Thorn’s CSAM (Child Sexual Abuse Material) detection software (Safer Match) are all viable and tested candidates for this detection. This detection ideally comes after the creation of AI generated content but before the presentation of that content to the user.

**(b) Requirement for Sexually Explicit Child Content Detection in Open-Source Distribution:**

All open-source projects which contribute to the creation of AI-generated deepfakes and content must employ the content detection systems referenced in Section 2(a). Open-source platforms and distributors, such as GitHub, must amend their community contribution guidelines to specifically prohibit the publication of open-source AI-content generation projects which fail to employ these content detection systems aimed at reducing the sexual exploitation of children.

**SECTION 3. RESTRICTIONS ON ACCESS OF SYNTHETIC MEDIA INVOLVING MINORS**

**(a) Age Restriction for Use of Deep Fake Engines and Open-Source Deepfake Projects**
Individuals under the age of 13 are prohibited from using any engine specifically designed for the creation of synthetic media. Existing deepfake creation engines and deepfake creation engines created in the future must add mechanisms to enforce a minimum age requirement.

**SECTION 4. PENALTIES AND ENFORCEMENT**

**(a) Criminal Penalties**:
Any individual or entity found guilty of knowingly creating or distributing synthetic explicit content involving minors shall be subject to imprisonment for 10-30 years in prison. Additional fines may be imposed in addition to prison time. More severe penalties may be levied in the case of multiple offenses or prior convictions.

**(b) Federal Trade Commission (FTC) Enforcement**:
The FTC shall have the authority to investigate and impose penalties on any creators of deepfake or AI-media generation engines found in violation of Section 2(a) of this act and any open-source platforms or distributors found in violation of 2(b) of this Act.

**(c) Civil Action**:
Parents or guardians of minors depicted in synthetic explicit content may seek to bring action for general or special damages against the individual or entity responsible for creating or distributing the synthetic material, as well as the proprietors of the engine used to generate the synthetic material. The court may also award costs and attorney fees to the plaintiff.

**SECTION 5. RULEMAKING AND ENACTMENT**

Within six months after enactment, the FTC and Department of Justice shall establish the regulation necessary to enforce this Act, including age-verification and content compliance procedures for open-source platforms.

This Act shall take effect six months after the date of enactment.
