# You Don't Own Me — Kindred essay
*Rhea Madhogarhia — Period 9, 1/29/2018 (HS English, recovered from Google Drive).*

Dana prompts Tom Weylin's fury through words and actions by establishing herself as an actual person rather than property that he owns. Dana is called to Rufus again while he is drowning during a storm, in mud, drunk, sick, and wet. Dana rescues him alongside Nigel and is back at the Weylin plantation. After she takes Rufus up to his bed she talks with Tom Weylin. Dana is thanking Weylin for the letter he sent that brought her and her husband, Kevin back together but Weylin neglects that his kindness is directed towards Dana. In response, Dana yells, "I don't give a damn why you did it! I'm just telling you, one human being to another, that I'm grateful. Why can't you leave it at that!" (200). Dana is showing Weylin that she is not his property or a slave by being disrespectful, comparing each other as equals, and lashing out. She is disrespectful when she uses the word "damn" because she is cursing and doing something that slaves would probably get beat for. Dana is showing that she can say whatever she wants to Weylin because she is as much of a person as Tom is. She can control her own emotions and behavior, unlike other slaves he owns, which he proceeds to treat like objects or property. Dana is comparing Tom to herself when she uses the words "human being" because she is differentiating herself from a slave or a piece of Weylin property. She is saying that there are two human beings in a conversation right now and that she is, in fact, human and not inferior to any white man. She is using the mindset from her own time periods to say that everyone on this planet is a human being, therefore are all equal.

Later in the conversation Dana threatens to leave Rufus alone if beaten or dehumanized. Tom calls her crazy but Dana knows that he needs her for Tom's son to stay alive. She replies by saying, "At the rate Mister Rufus has accidents… he might live another six or seven years without me. I wouldn't count on more than that" (201). Dana is making herself and her presence in Rufus's life valued and important. When Dana estimates Rufus's life expectancy as another six or seven years she is also insinuating that Rufus will die young because of his own mistakes. Then later in the sentence she says that this will happen only when Dana is gone which means the only reason why Tom's son is alive is because she has saved his life countless times. Tom and Rufus see Dana as inferior because she is a black woman, a person that would usually be treated as property, but Dana disproves her inferiority by saying that she is needed in Rufus's life in order for him to live. In fact Rufus dies because of his own mistakes which makes Dana smarter and superior to Rufus, a white man whose father owns slaves. Tom is enraged by this because slaves are not valued or respected as people because they are uneducated and not allowed to think for themselves, so Dana displaying her opinion on Tom shouldn't be something that's allowed to happen.
