3) How does domain expertise (accrued via experience) change one's working memory
and/or short-term memory? Or more broadly, what factors influence the individual
differences in working memory and/or short-term memory that we see across individuals?
The core reading (“The Mind and Brain of Short-Term Memory”) discusses how working
memory and short-term memory consist of activated long-term memory representations and the allocation
of attention. In lecture, Prof Heald discussed the idea that we have limits on our STM capacity but these
limits can be stretched by techniques like chunking, which, according to the textbook, is a process
possible through accessing LTM stores crafted by a person's domain expertise and experience. One
example in the textbook that shows the correlation between knowledge accessed from LTM being used to
increase our ability to hold STM info through chunking is Erricson’s experiment on patient S.F. Patient
S.F initially had a very average/normal memory span, but through extensive training, was able to increase
his digit/memory span to around 79 digits. He did this through chunking techniques that gave sequences
of numbers meaning, essentially taking multiple units of information and condensing them into a single
unit that, through accessing his LTM and expertise/prior knowledge, he could decode back into multiple
units when asked. For example, Patient S.F was a runner and so compiling number sequences into race
times made the multiple numbers easier to remember - remembering a single time rather than 6-9
different numbers. This shows that individuals can develop unique long-term memory representations in
their area of expertise that can be more readily activated and maintained in working and short term
memory. Another example that Professor Heald touched on was that chess experts have memory
advantages for placing chess positions due to their extensive knowledge. While Prof Heald hinted that
sometimes this makes chess experts ill prepared against complete beginners because of the randomness of
their moves, chess experts are, nonetheless, calling upon their LTM to access expertise in their domain
and manipulate the information from their sensory memory in their working memory.
Attention control is another key factor influencing working memory capacity. Individuals with
better executive control can maintain focus (selectively attend) and resist interference, keeping relevant
information accessible despite distractions. Vogel’s event related potential experiment demonstrated
attention control’s effect on working memory capacity well: when two groups (one with a high working
memory capacity and one with a low WM capacity) were asked to detect the orientation change of red
bars in an image filled with red and blue bars, the high working memory capacity group was better at
selectively attending to the changes in the red bars whereas the blue bars were taking up more space in the
low working memory capacity group causing their judgment/decision time in the experiment to be longer.
.
Overall, we see that domain expertise provides knowledge representations to draw upon and
specialized encoding strategies that boost working memory while attention control abilities determine
how well individuals can maintain focus and resist interference from irrelevant information. Working
memory is not only important in having a more in depth and nuanced understanding of STM and LTM,
but also in people understanding how they learn today. Just recently I had a conversation with a friend
about working memory because I believed she had a high capacity working memory based on what we
had talked about in lecture. It turns out she was diagnosed with having an “unreliable working memory”
when she was being tested for learning disabilities. While this was her medical diagnosis, she had great
insight into how it played into her academic vs. social life and how her working memory in different
scenarios (namely scenarios she felt more comfortable in) changes: she is really good at remembering

details in social conversations and applying callbacks to that information but academically falls short.
This makes sense as she is a person that puts extra effort into her social relationships and sees herself as a
“people” person. Beginning to understand how your mind works can be very empowering and
recognizing what you attend to, forget, and remember can be a great way to figure out how to best serve
and feed your mind.
