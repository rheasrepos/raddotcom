It's 4:30 am on Monday, July 27th, so the 26th, and I just finished watching [[Pluribus]]. 

there are a few obvious things we can talk about, like [[AI]] or what rich people think communism is, but I think the point of pluribus, and it was honestly there all along (i think they did a good job of that), is to show that even when the collective is strong, bountiful, peaceful, good, ethical, humane, and nonexhaustively deeply moraled, there is something about the human, and *only* the human as a species, that makes them still crave something more in their lives. What that something more is is not the experience of your personal life, but your interior personal life, your [[consciousness]]. 

It doesn't surprise me that [[Apple]] is producing a show about how the most mainstream and popular theory of interior and personal life is the thing we should think about as [[humanity]] in this day and age: privacy and encryption, digital personas and profiles that are highly personalized. Apple cares about you thinking that your [[individuality]] is what makes you more human than the collective, although they do that... as a collective of industries and professionals and talents. They are an amazing company, in many ways i think its ethos is embedded in the ideals of [[cognitive science]]. 



Computer Science: They believe in a computational understanding of their users. As is typical of any tech company. The value behind [[computational theory of the mind]] is kind of a given. 

Psychology: The [[user experience]] is what makes them Apple and requires understanding of our behaviors and habits. They cracked that. They made a really good product that we all love. i love my phone. And specifically my iPhone, doesn't matter the version. 

Neuroscience: Apple has already branched into biotech and wearables and that shows us that they deeply care about your body (data) and health (heart rate monitoring, etc) 

Linguistics: SIRI! They've been ***doing*** [[natural language processing]]. Apple has distinct prose. This has always been true. Not sure how to describe it, but it has a minimalistic but curious tone, a smart choice to allow the user to create their own attachment to the interface rather than force one upon the user. 

Philosophy: And now they need your mind. They need you to be deeply invested and aligned with a philosophy that benefits you using this technology despite all of the bugs, market decisions, and addictive interactions it exposes you to. They want to produce a show that makes you question science fiction, ponder a future that may be manifested biologically or computationally, make you seriously consider the benefits of The Joined, and convince you that even in that world where The Joined happens, you'll be fine because your individuality will shine through and you won't give up on saving the world. You'll do it!!! 


So my problem here is that.... the show seems deeply invested in the personal defining our collective, but where was that attitude in that government lab that decided to replicate a DNA sequence from an extraterrestrial technosignature? Where in that factory line lab with researchers that probably really care about science and their jobs and their humanity, was the personal accountability, on A SINGLE PERSON, to raise the faintest tiniest sliver of an idea that "it might not be good to replicate something we think is a fucking RNA sequence.....". They probably wouldn't say "fucking" but it felt appropriate for me. Honestly, this makes me think that Pluribus is commentary on our current world already being tainted by the destructive behavioral habits AI and integrated technology use is only going to worsen, and then THE JOINED is a good, awesome AI world, but Carol Sturka is the one that reminds the "fixed" AI world that humanity has been fundamentally lost because of the eradication of the inner self. 

The only thing Apple is wrong about there is that the eradication of the inner self is not as big of a threat as the eradication of our grace towards the collective. 

The agenda Apple pushes with Pluribus is that "look, we know AI is gonna fuck things up in a bad but also sick and cool way..... BUTTTT don't worry because humanity always wins <3"

Honestly, it's instilling a bubble of hope I think is worth popping. 
Technology has made us more self-aware, more self-centered (not a negative connotation, maybe self-preserving is more right?), and thus, anxious, tired, empowered, and energized all at the same time. Our individuality is actually the only thing we share when we use technology. A human in contact with an artifact is an expression of individuality within humanity. A human in contact with a human is a piece of the collective history of humanity. In a way, it probably defines humanity. Humans do not exist without each other existing.

Individuality is a trend on Instagram. I remember i followed suit in high school when I realized people around me were casual posting on main and not curating their tile feed. That was awesome for me becuase the vsco was tiring to be honest. But, in college I've noticed that there is pressure to be yourself online, when really the internet is a really awesome avenue to express something other than yourself. The World Wide Web seems like the perfect place to curate a collective mind.... and it has. Large Language Models have taken the text we've digitally created to create a language for itself to speak to us in. That's so awesome. But that's also why we can tell that AI text is AI text when we hear it. Because for the people who built the internet up until this point. You, me, your friends, your mom and dad, your neighbor, kind of anyone above the age of 18 in 2026, we were NOT ourselves online. We were a polished, annoying, funny, optimistic, cynical version of ourselves.... different whenever we wanted to be. AI chatbots have a lot of those traits if you prompt it a certain way.... and those are really cool things to be able to mimic with language (AND COMPLICATED). But obviously the internet is changing rapidly, and internet speak isn't formal enough for a chatbot to model off of. So our sociocultural linguistic changes travel faster amongst us than through web scraping and training data updates to large language models. But the tone of AI WILL change because our speech will change too. Our individuality will ALWAYS be reflected in technology if we remain the users of it. What changes is our awareness of the collective power when the collective presence is non-digital. When we think about ourselves as a collective of separated individuals, a collective defined by their awareness of one another rather than defined as their connection to one another, the collective that happens at the species - human - maybe surival- level, then we realize how much harder it is to stay a collective than to be just an individual. When we completely let go of our physical and social awareness of each other, in cities, and rooms, and streets, we become bodies. We become bumbling idiots that have perfectly normal, maybe even above average motor skills. We fall, and we get hurt, and we realize it's become hard to ask for help or support. Asking of someone's presence is no longer normal. We can text! We try and find a stranger and feel uncomfortable doing so, because of the situation or their demeanor or the lack of strangers around. We'd probably call someone we know anyways because the people around me wouldn't care anyway, they have no reason to care about me because they don't know me. I would do the same!"

And then we really wish sometimes that we knew everyone in a crowd. You remember moshing for the first time even though you hated it and didn't know it was gonna happen and were wearing a skimpy bandana as a top but you smiled the whole time. You remember dancing to Olivia Dean at Lollapalooza because it was crazy she was there and being around people that were straight VIBING the whole time while like Kesha or someone else was on the mainstage. You remember every bathroom line conversation you've ever had. You remember an old "Trump hating" man sitting next to you at Pizzana at dinner and telling you every joke in his arsenal. Sweet, but they were deeply misogynistic and unfunny.

You remember people caring about you when they didn't have to. Or at least you remember being cared for when maybe today you'd think it's uncalled for"


ESSENTIALLY you'd remember that feeling like a part of something, whatever that is, is human. When you care about people for the sole sake of them being another human being, when you care about people for the sole reason that you are aware of them, you admit to yourself that something you're doing ought to be shared, and important, even if that "doing" is just "being". 


Alright so back to Pluribus. I think the characters are special because they are all characters who are SO BAD AT BEING. They're all like work freaks or asian parents, or masochists, or indulgent assholes. And that's why the message is all wrong, because all of the characters embody the wrong thing about humanity that needs to be saved; it isn't the individual, it's the collective when faced with the fact that every part of the collective is fundamentally separated. The only thing binding them is choice. The choice to notice. 


Some of you might say Carol WANTS to save HUMANITY. She wants to save the collective!! Ok, but this isn't a collective v collective show. Carol Sturka is annoying and unlikable because she cares about the populous in so far as much as it benefits her ego. She's a self-deprecating author who finds no joy or pride in writing books that she has dedicated loyal fans for. Carol wants to save herself. The ONly reason Carol left Zosia was that she found out that they were still gonna convert her. IF she knew that she could stay unjoined forever, she would never have gone back to "saving the world". Mansousu refuses to believe the new reality he lives in and wants things to change "back" to the way they were, although that will truly never happen. He denies any humanity to The Joined, although they do resemble much of humanity. As much of humanity as an LLM does, but in a human body and the ability to feel---arguably a new species or type of human - it is genetic alteration after all. Every 12-year-old girl is self-centered, but also she kinda had no agency anyways. Vegas Guy was honestly Carroll Sturka coded. He'd never cross over, but he respects their existence because it awards his dream lifestyle. Lakshmi cares about her stupid genius boy too much to want to cause him any harm or turbulence. And I don't remember the rest. 


I'm getting tired of this essay, and it's 5:27 am, so let me try and wrap up. Pluribus is a show about how the benefits of the collective entice us, yet still we can't fight the feeling that there is something about individual selves that truly defines us as human and thus is indispensable.  

Pluribus is a show produced by Apple, a company that profits off of the idea that you think your personal ife is deeply important and more valuable than a general experience. A tailored life is a better one. They can cater their products to this demographic using AI and other personalization techniques. 

Pluribus is also a show about modern development of science and technology is catastrophic, but there's hope that we'll get out of it if that does happen. Apple is a company that wants you to think that adoption of this technology, an Apple intelligence experiment or chatbot, is beneficial in the long run and that in the end, this technology will make our lives better. 

Pluribus is a show that fiercely promotes the preservation of humanity but characterizes humanity in a highly presumptuous way. That it is our individuality that defines us is assumed. That it is the collection of individuals only connected by perception is deeply, deeply missed. That is the aspect of humanity we ought to champion as the world changes around us. If not economically or politically, at least in a socio-cultural way. 

Anyways, Pluribus was a beautifully shot show with a crazy budget, it seems, and a very thought-provoking yet underexplained premise. You can disagree with me, but you gotta convince me.
