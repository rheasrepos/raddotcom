TEACHING PHILOSOPHY STATEMENT

My drive to learn how to teach stems from the idea that kids minds are
best fostered when taken seriously. I almost view this as, don't
underestimate a student's learning or pre-existing capabilities. From my
point of view, the basic way in which we learn is super similar to how
young kids learn. We are able to learn our first language from birth and
reach fluency practically by the time we start elementary schooling. I
think this drives me to approach teaching as a way of molding an
instructional approach through an evolving understanding how your
students learn rather than teaching material and forcing kids to learn
it in the way you desire to lecture or teach it. When a kid "doesn't get
it", I hope not just to repeat myself, or preach "practice", but to ask,
what about it do you not understand.

This approach obviously requires deep commitment to students and
expertise in the area you are teaching. I think often teachers default
to the high level idea to explain a concept, but when I think about how
many of my peers/I best learn, we reach understanding by connecting a
larger idea to its constitutive parts. On the students part, this also
requires respect and understanding of their peers learning process,
patience in the classroom, and collaboration. I hope to inspire students
to help each other and model a way of teaching that students can
replicate and aid their peers with out of the desire to have a shared
understanding of the world and the material being taught.

I think that my teaching practice will be developed through a series of
trial and error. Obviously throughout this process, even the "errors"
should have some sort of contribution to student's learning. When
teaching English as a native speaker, I hope to be casual and
approachable in my speech, speaking in a way that I can always expect or
encourage a response to. These responses will serve as informal
assessments and formal assessments should serve more as a
notice/progress check to the students themselves rather than a grade
indicator for myself. While in practice I do not know what this looks
like yet, compassion, patience, curiosity, and collaboration are all
central to my teaching philosophy and are all values I hope to inspire
in my students as well.
