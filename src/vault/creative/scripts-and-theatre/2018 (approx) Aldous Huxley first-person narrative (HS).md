Aldous Huxley Narrative
My name is Aldous Huxley. I was born in Godalming, England on July 26, 1894. My mother was Julia Arnold, a schoolmistress, and my father was Leonard Huxley, a schoolmaster and editor. Also, my grandfather was a well-known zoologist. Therefore, I was expected to be just as intelligent as the rest of my family. When I was a boy, I wanted to become a scientist. However, when I was a teenager, I almost went blind, and had poor eyesight for the rest of my life. My condition (Keratitis Punctata) ruined any dream I had of becoming a scientist. Not only was I nearly blind, but my entire youth was very tragic. My mother died of cancer and my brother Noel committed suicide. 
I attended the prestigious preparatory school, Eton. Later, I attended Balliol College, Oxford and graduated in 1916. Three years later, I married Maria Nys and the birth of my son Matthew followed this a year later. 
I wrote my first novel, Crome Yellow in 1921. In 1932, I published my most well-known novel, Brave New World. In Brave New World, I stated that scientific progress was harmful, and in the future we would only care about machines and science, not individuality. 
Finally, in 1937 I immigrated to America due to the fact that it had nicer weather, and war was possible in Europe. In 1938 I moved to Hollywood and added “screenwriter” to my long list of careers. I wrote many plays such as Jane Eyre, Marie Curie, and Pride and Prejudice. 
On February 12, 1955, my wife Maria died, and later I married Laura Archera. Life was very different in America than in England. For instance, in America, people seemed to value their possessions more than each other.
Throughout my life in the United States, I tried multiple times to apply for citizenship. However, I became a Pacifist after World War I. This created many obstacles. However, another thing that created obstacles with the government was that if war occurred, I was not willing to enlist in the army. Therefore, I stayed an immigrant for all of my life.
In 1960, I was diagnosed with cancer. Soon after, I wrote my final book, The Island. This book was a reflection of Brave New World, more optimistic. I realized that people still had a chance to change the world, it didn’t have to turn out the way it did in Brave New World. There was still hope for the future of mankind.
I died on November 22, 1963. I had written over 50 books, ranging from travel books to novels. I had written what might have been considered one of the greatest books of the 20th century. In all of my life, I had made many great contributions to America, though I was not born in America.
