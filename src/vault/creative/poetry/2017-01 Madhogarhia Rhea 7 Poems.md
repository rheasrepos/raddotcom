Morning Identities

Pink streaks and orange horizon

Amber rays shine upon my 

Bundle of blankets

Eyes open as I wake and 

My system is in full action

I get ready and my brain is bewildered

Staring at a weighted wardrobe 

full of colors, shapes, and designs

This decision lays my whole day in front of me

Weather, style, comfort, and...

Personality

My clothes allow me to change my personality

 A dress,

elegant and a bit risky

Shorts, 

 A stylish addition to any guise

Sweats,

 the spitting image of comfort

From now till night I could be a student,

party animal, procrastinator,

Or the person who just doesn’t care

Who expresses themself how they want to

I can choose anything, and I have,

But everyday I choose to dress someway 

different

But that doesn't change who

I AM

The Mango Tango

Glowing red,

 freckles of yellow

Fading down to an emerald green 

a dress like feathers of a tropical bird

Brown scars and dents displaying every fall, 

Scratch,

Bruise

Stamped with a colorful seal representing its country of Peru

smooth sensation

Like a wax figure is sensed as I find my way through it 

It's as soft as a bunny as I cut across the floor with it

I can see inside it as I dance and dance 

With the sharpness of a knife.

Its emotion and soul pouring out in front of me 

A round character with slight imperfections

A bright and happy feeling, it gives me the sense of yellow

Yet there is a brown to it, a hardness and sincereness

darkside

But it’s sleekness makes all that go away

Savoring the moment is what makes the mango tango special

Pancake 

All heads up examining the ball contact 

the hands of the opposing player as it spirals down with force 

One player stoops low to the ground

The ball beckons down faster and faster

The player obtains a quizzical look on her face

Again looking up, ball spins and whirls for a thousand revolutions

She lunges forward with the ball still a few feet in front of her

And plunges her body onto the floor 

Kneepads grazing the surface

Now sprawled on the floor 

she lengthens her hand flat out in front of her

Her head on the ground now, eyes closed, 

only trusting her body 

3 inches, 2 inches, 1 inch 

It touches her fingertip and 

soars up higher than the net, as the force rushes through her hand

Just enough for her team to pass it up, and bash it down

She hears 1 clap, 5 claps, soon too many to cipher

She glances over and smiles

Lamplight

It sits still in my room 

Never being bothered

Until it is dark and still 

It lights up my place

And brings joy to my face

Like a firefly in the pitch black of night 

Giving joy to children and light for the animals

It came from our ancestors as a blazing hot glow

But this fire has evolved through decades 

And is now sitting on my shelf

With a shiny base and stem 

That’s hot to the touch

Hot enough that it doesn't burn you

But instead brings you warmth when near

It is not an object of destruction or heaviness,

But an object of light, in many more ways than one

For it is the lamplight that rides next to my bed

1. I think that I am best at using and incorporating imagery in my poems. I use similes, metaphors and sensory detail the best. I realized that I like to describe stuff in a fun and creative way like in my grapefruit poem, I used sensory detail and a metaphor in the same line when I said, “ enclosing teardrops of bittersweet”. I was not only describing the looks and the taste of the grapefruit but also comparing it to a teardrop. Sometimes I think that metaphors are more sophisticated because it is not obviously pointing out what you are referring to but it is still clear. I think I use similes just as well because they are easier to incorporate or when I think I am writing a metaphor it turns out that it’s a simile instead. Sometimes that I have used similes is in my swan poem when I wrote “It sits like a pearl in the sea”. I like to use similes because they add some fun to our poems and lets people see a simple object in a way they never thought they would’ve. 

2. One tool that I didn’t use as much as I should have is compression. When I am writing I find that making complete thoughts leaves people with a satisfied idea in their mind. When I take a word out of a sentence such as them or a, it could sometimes change the meaning of the whole line, so whenever I see a line that could be compressed, I do it. But if it is a sentence which has a few tiny words but it’s still a meaningful line where every word has a purpose, then I will have no reason to change it. I think compressing your poem may sometimes compress your ideas and make you want to change things unnecessarily, at least for me it does. That is why I don’t think compression is that useful. 

Another thing that I didn’t use that much was alliteration or word play. When I think of word play, I think of a really fun poem or line that adds some humor or lightheartedness to it. When I start to explain something it is not in a cheery fashion or in a sad way but more of a deep explanation that is read with heart. I don’t think wordplay necessarily fits with my types of poem but it is still something I try to do sometimes. If I wrote the cliche line, “as dark as night”, I would not want to write “a dark dusk”, because it loses the meaning and it doesn’t fit well. If I have to go out of my way to find a way to use wordplay it will be forced and will not flow well with the poem. I think that when you read a poem it should have a certain rhythm and flow to it.

3. One thing that I have realized about myself as a poet is that I don’t tend to write necessarily happy or upbeat poems. My poems aren’t sad or depressing, it’s just that they have deep meanings and a more slow and meaningful rhythm. I write about happy topics but I don’t use words that are usually associated with “happy”. I tend to write those typical lines that use lines like “I stepped into the darkness” or another (I’ll use the word again) cliché line like that because I feel that I need to be poetic but I’ve realized that any series of phrases can really be a poem. For example the poem of all made up words, “The Loch Ness Monster’s Song”. I think I have started away from doing that type of writing where everything has to be flowery, like I'm trying to hard for it to be a poem. That doesn’t mean that I will stop doing it, but I thought it was interesting how my idea of a poem is completely different from already great poems out there. That is something I have learned about myself writing poetry, and poetry itself.
