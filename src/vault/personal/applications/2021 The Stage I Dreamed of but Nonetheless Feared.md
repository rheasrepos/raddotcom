# The Stage I Dreamed of but Nonetheless Feared
*Rhea Madhogarhia — recovered from Google Drive (High School, ~2021). Personal essay.*

I feel uneasy on a stage. Camera off. My voice trembles in front of a crowd. Mic muted. I can't bear to see a real image of myself. Video mirrored. I've never found it easy to perform, yet I call myself a performer. My instrument of choice is my voice but I can't come to playback the recordings I submit to my choir teacher and doubt every note I produce as I rehearse for my solo in our online musical. Performing arts has always been a big deal here, so we modified; well they modified. Besides playing Wilbur in my third grade musical production of "The Charlotte's Web" I've never been in a musical. I've dreamt of being in one, but I've always had a thing. Something that stopped me from committing my time to something I knew would bring me immense happiness. Yet I chose the sport I loved so much, it destroyed every ounce of passion I carried for the game. Finally, I let go of that. It came naturally when our energizing taxing club practices rapidly transitioned and depreciated to lacking zoom meetings, void of effort or passion. Join meeting. I had always wanted free time but not like this. I was going to school like normal until one day in mid May I heard chanting, screaming, and cheering on "the Quad" as people were celebrating what was apparently our last day of in person classes. I had longed for a break from the mundane and exhausting cycle of sleep, school, practice, work, more practice, more work, repeat, but no one imagined we'd still be taking this "break" a year from now. I told myself there were silver linings, but I'm not sure how much those slivers of light could guide me through the darkness that crept in in the confines of my walls. School and rest welded together to form a heavy blanket, seemingly warm and comforting but consuming my room, my bed, my dreams. I realized that my silver linings had been peeled off of the beige crown molded ceilings, my sky. As time passed, unnatural circadian rhythms dictating what I experienced as a day rather than the light outside my windows, Fall seeped in and I noticed a crack in the roof of my cozy yet smothering vault. I couldn't go outside. I so desperately wanted to go outside; then I felt the air filling my lungs for the first time as I heard the familiar ring of my email and glanced at the subject line of my most recent message: "HW 2020/2021 Musical - STEPHEN SCHWARTZ: ALL THE LIVELONG DAY". This was my chance. I didn't have to audition in front of a room of judging students and I didnt have to sacrifice the clubs and time I spent on homework every night. When I received a callback my heart began racing, not only because it was the only news I'd wanted to hear for weeks, but because I feared what would come next. I launched the meeting and was met by two incredibly friendly directors who tested the highs, lows, color, and temperament of my voice. I thought it was over until I was asked to sing the song I had deliberately chosen not to submit. I knew they had said it was because they thought I could deliver it ably but as my mind grew hyper the world moved slower and I was stuck in place believing I was sinking. Join meeting without video. I avoid turning my camera on when I first join a meeting. I avoid those first few seconds where my 16x9 box is visible to everyone but my laggy screen. Join meeting with audio. I collect myself in the moments before I present myself. Video on. After greetings and small talk I press play on the audition track and open my mouth to produce the C# that begins the melody. The calmness I collected moments earlier is gone. My control over my arms, neck, face, and feet are gone. I am not grounded; I am stuck standing stiff and straight in front of my dresser carefully attempting to learn the lyrics as I went. I hear absolutely nothing but I know I am making sound. I thought this time would be different, that I would be comfortable in the room that I belt and sing in every moment I am not in school but the same hauntingly familiar feeling took over. My parents say it comes with practice, but I've been practicing since I was 9. Mute. They gave me such a supportive and glowing review but I can never tell online….if they're just being... polite. For everyone else, the callback was the easy part. The real hard part was waiting for the solo list. I kept my expectations low because I was new to this but I secretly wished I didn't get one. I didn't want to have to relive that zoom experience a hundred more times over the next few months. I hear my email ring again and I mindlessly open the message to see my name next to the song they made me sing at the audition. My name was trailed with whitespace around songs listed with three or four other names on them. I was doing it alone. I was congratulated by upperclassmen, friends, and my brother but they knew that I was in distress. I didn't know why they chose me. Well I knew why; they liked my audition, but I couldn't recall what they liked about my audition because I had not one positive thought about my performance. I remember the directors asking me what I thought I did well after I sang it. My mind only allowed me to say that I was on pitch; the directors laughed and reassured me there was more, but I didn't believe them. There were months of joining meetings in the comfort of my room and singing while muted. I practiced the songs by myself, with my voice teacher, around my brother, but every time it came to rehearsing a solo, either my solo or a group one, I relived that first experience. No matter how comfortable I was with the cast, I and the people that knew me could hear the tremor in my voice. I knew that when it came to putting the musical together, I would be doing it alone in my room on my own time. Unmuted and unplugged from a zoom server and singing for only myself. Even with that reassurance of a good final product, I felt disappointment more than anything. Was I wasting rehearsal time if I wasn't improving in their eyes? How can I force myself to forget they're there and show them what I know? When will I be able to accept compliments without doubting their intentions or feeling that they're out of pity? Looking back, I never got to answer any of those questions, but I did show them what I could do. In those last two stressful months I got every recording and video submission in on time and made them the best I could. After months of singing ensemble pieces on mute I wanted to surprise them, make them realize - no, make myself realize that I was valuable in this space. Online school made this feel impossible. Everything I had worked for since the beginning of the semester, both academically and non academically was culminating in early 2021. Endless arrays of assignments due a minute before midnight, weekends filled with half day rehearsals, academic competitions, and commitments I was too attached to ease up on. I told myself, next month will make it all worth it. I often make this insane and unreasonable trade off with myself. Do the work now so you'll be less stressed later. The problem is, with these endless work days, the stress is always looming and the reality becomes bear twice the strain now so you can be regularly stressed later. Sometimes I listen to this reasoning and stop to let myself enjoy a moment outside with my dog and leave the flammable pile of work for later. It will only burn if I strike the match, endure the heat tomorrow and bask in the coolness of today. Upload files. Even though everyone had their own meeting times, I'd get nervous when the rehearsal schedule had an allotted time with only my name on it. I'd go in wondering if I needed to redo the work I'd submitted the week before, fix notes or parts in my vocals, or if they thought my acting was too unskilled and juvenile to be used. Living my life online has made me hold myself to an unusual standard. I desire to do more than is physically possible, to work in hours of the day that I don't have, and to feel productive when there is nothing left to produce. When you're home all the time, when you can be in a room with 20 people at the click of a button, it feels like you have no excuse to be idle. Simply being feels like a choice you have to make and doing is the default. Arguably it should be the opposite but my mind tells me that's an excuse for not doing enough and this constant debate with myself leaves me stuck. Join Meeting. I heard only positive feedback the last two weeks of rehearsal. I was happy to hear they were pleased with my punctual submissions and was satisfied with this surface level praise because I told myself that I couldn't anticipate them to expect for me what I expected for myself. I virtually stepped into my rehearsal with our music director and found myself filled with pure content and relief. For the first time in this process, I did not doubt the intention behind the feedback I was receiving. In rehearsals there were little corrections but also little praise from anyone. The instrumental silence in rehearsal could be split with a knife but I was scared of what was hiding in the inside of that cut so I never dared to test it. This made their content with the recordings I'd submitted all the more satisfying. There didn't have to be theatrics or enthusiasm because even a murmur of gratitude or congratulation spoke volumes more than silence. I took the complement to heart for possibly the first time. The tone of surprise in their voice showed me that I was successful in showing them what I couldn't in those moments of freezing anxiety on screen. I don't think I can say that I believed in myself from that point forward and my stage fright hasn't vanished, but I relished in the weightlessness of that evening for the first time in a long time. I'll continue to have hotspots in my timeline but I won't stop working to carve out the moments where I can bask in the coolness.
